The German verb 'melden' is very handy

Wednesday, August 19, 2026

  • german

As I'm writing this article, I've received all the necessary documents to move to Austria and even booked the tickets. I hope to set foot in Austria on the 3rd of September 2026.

The verb melden always managed to confuse me during my B2 days. In some places it meant to report, whereas elsewhere sich melden meant to raise your hand/speak up??? Endlich habe ich mich entschieden, es richtig gut zu recherchieren, und die verschiedenen Bedeutungen richtig zu beherrschen.

The Core Idea

The core idea of melden is to make information/presence known. The prefixes then tell what happens to the information or presence.

There is information → You make someone aware of it.

In that sense, melden means to report/notify/let someone know. It can also be seen used in apps such as Instagram to report an Account to Meta as inappropriate or such.

Ich melde den Account bei Meta als unangemessen. (I report the account to Meta as inappropriate.)

Ich melde das Problem meinem Chef. (I report the problem to my boss.)

Here, it is etwas jemandem melden.

'sich' made it spicier

sich melden as a verb literally means to make oneself known.

When you say:

Ich melde mich.

It literally translates to:

I'm making myself known.

And here's the part that used to confused the fuck out of me. Depending on context, the meaning becomes:

  1. I'll get in touch.
  2. I'll let you know.
  3. I'm speaking up.
  4. I'm checking in.
  5. I'm putting my hand up.

WHAAAA???

Let's see a few examples to understand it.

Example 1

Lehrer: Wer kennt die Antwort?

Du: Ich melde mich!

Here, sich melden means to signal that you want to speak, essentially I'm putting my hand up.

Example 2

A friend sends you a message and is waiting for your answer.

Ich melde mich morgen bei dir. (I'll make myself known to you tomorrow.)

Sounds weird, but this roughly translates to:

I'll get in touch with you tomorrow.

See how useful sich melden is?

Prefixes are not very complicated

AN und AB

Imagine a database containing a list of people. You are not in the list yet.

sich anmelden gives the idea of entering/being added to the list, which roughly translates to to register/sign up/log in.

Now you are on the list.

sich abmelden is to leave the list, which roughly translates to, you guessed it: to deregister/withdraw/log out.

This works beautifully with courses.

With your ever-growing thirst to learn the language, you say:

Ich melde mich für den Deutschkurs bei der Universität an. (I'm signing up for the German course.)

für if for what you are registering for. bei is for who you are registering with.

After encountering Adjektivendugen, you decide you've suffered enough and say:

Ich melde mich von dem Deutschkurs ab. (I'm withdrawing from the German course.)

Similarly, von is the preposition here (withdraw from something).

ZURÜCK

This is the easiest one.

zurück means back. So, sich zurückmelden means to make oneself known back. Therefore:

Ich melde mich zurück.

means:

I'll get back to you

Example 1

You got a mail saying

Can you check this and let me know?

You don't have the answer yet.

You say:

Ich melde mich später zurück. (I'll get back to you later.)

KRANK

sich krankmelden literally means to report oneself sick.

You can say:

Ich muss mich heute krankmelden. (I have to call in sick today.)

Here, melden is to notify, and krank is sick. You are basically notifying that you are sick.

WEITER

weitermelden means to pass/report something onward.

Example 1

Suppose your colleague tells you:

Der Kunde hat die Bestellung abgesagt.

You need to tell this to your boss.

Ich melde das meinem Chef weiter. (I'll pass that information on to my boss.)

For everyday German, just stick with weiter|geben.
